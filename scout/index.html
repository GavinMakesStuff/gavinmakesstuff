Why I built this

A resume mistake I didn't even know I was making.

Years ago, I built a resume using my very poorly developed graphic design skills. In my attempt to stand out and be unique, I overdesigned the heck out of it, making it completely unreadable to an ATS. I didn't even know ATS systems existed at the time. I applied to about 50 jobs, some of which I was genuinely qualified for, but heard back from no one.

I eventually landed a job at a student job board (how ironic!), which is where I first learned what an ATS actually was. Even after that, knowing better, I still kept running into trouble. I later found out companies like Workday can keep a resume on file for up to three years, so that one bad resume may have quietly been disqualifying me from jobs long after I thought I'd fixed the problem (they're in legal trouble over this now, BIG surprise).

That mistake probably cost me some real opportunities early in my career, and frankly, that pissed me off. And I'm petty. So I built Scout.

I built Scout to help try to avoid that from happening to others. Scout isn't an ATS checker. That market's already crowded. Instead, I built it during my next job search, when I came across a new problem: reading through long, dense postings only to realize three paragraphs in that I was missing one core requirement. I was also tracking all my applications, or companies I found appealing, in a messy spreadsheet.

This app aims to minimize that wasted time and provides tracking for applications so I don't have to rely on spreadsheets. I basically just took everything that was frustrating me about searching for half-decent job postings and threw it all together in one app that made sense for me.

I figure if I've had this problem, so do others, and that's why I built Scout.

Gavin, Creator of Scout
